I am a firm believer in olfactory senses having a distinct yet indiscernible link to memory. One normal day you are walking your normal route, doing something mundane on autopilot, and suddenly, seemingly from nowhere, a smell stops you in your tracks and teleports you to a hidden-away childhood memory, unlocking it for a fleeting moment. And then it's gone. But boy are you glad it upended your routine.

The thing is though, we don't get to choose when and how these links are made. We make plans to visit new places, consciously choose to make new memories of people and things. But smells, they make their way in unfettered. And they choose how long they want to linger. You look at a photo of a rainforest and see it plainly for what it is - dewy trees and tropical birds. But you only fathom the entirety of its existence when you experience its sensory offerings. The birds fluttering and calling above, as you brush against the dripping leaves and feel the rain on your skin. But none of those senses come remotely close to transporting you to another realm as much as the smell of the wet ground beneath. All of a sudden, you're a teenager back in your grandmother's backyard, hurriedly taking clothes off of the clothesline because nature had other plans.

The sound of rain hitting my windows is one of my favorite sounds. It might be yours too. But every now and then, leave that window a little ajar to let the smell in. You never know, it might end up being your new favorite memory in the making.
